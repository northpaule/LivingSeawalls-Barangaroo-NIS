Introduction

This project is part of the Living Seawalls project with the Sydney Institute of Marine Science (SIMS) and particularly aims to reduce the number of non-indigenous species (NIS) on panels through underwater gardening (hand removal) of two key ascidians NIS: Botrylloides leachii and Styela plicata, at Watermans Cove in Barangaroo, Sydney, NSW.

Note

The dataset initially appeared to contain duplicated panel numbers, suggesting that panel identifiers were inconsistent. However, the field protocol clarified that panel numbers were intentionally repeated across the three deployment depths (Intertidal, 1.2 m and 3 m). For example, Panel 102 exists once at each depth, and is only found on the North side of Watermans, representing three separate physical settlement panels rather than repeated records of the same panel. Similarly, Panel 21 exists once at each depth and is only found on the SR platform of the South side. While we expected to have 3 repeats of each panel number (128 panels x 3 depth positions = 384 panels sampled) per sampling period (384 panels sampled x 4 sampling periods = 1536 panels sampled across depth), NIS were only removed from half the panels at each depth (1568/2 = 768 observations). As such, our slightly lower total observation count of 701 (represented by 198 individual panels) is likely caused by experimental challenges such as missed observations and lost panels.

Therefore, this dataset represents only a subset of the original experiment and contains only the panels that were experimentally cleaned after each sampling event. Consequently, the unique experimental unit is not the panel number alone, but the combination of Span, Depth, and Panel number. A unique panel identifier (Panel_ID) was therefore created by combining these variables, allowing each physical panel to be tracked across repeated sampling periods while correctly accounting for repeated measurements in subsequent mixed-effects models.

Here, we look at linear, quadratic and cubic trends between mean Styela count (abundance) per panel across sampling periods, using a negative binomial GLMM. Styela abundance count increased significantly across sampling periods, but our model suggests that recolonization did not occur at a constant rate but rather in accelerated and varied bursts throughout the monitoring period (notably S1 and S4). Which is supported by our abundance plot.

GLMM was chosen here because it can analyze count data that are overdispered (as it is the case with our data), meaning that the variance is significantly greater than the mean. As a result, it prevents the underestimated standard errors and false-positive results that can occur when using a standard Poisson model (due to excess data variability).

  geom_bar() + theme_pub()
        # very crowded plot, but we can still detect a few key points...

Main patterns observed:

  • S1 and S2: Most observations are in the 0 category, meaning Botrylloides was absent from many panels. However, some small-to-medium colonies (S–L) were present, indicating early colonization.

  • S3: The proportion of 0 observations decreases, while XS, S, M, L, and XL colonies become more common. This suggests increasing recruitment and colony growth over time.

  • S4: There is a strong shift towards larger colonies, particularly L and XL size classes, with fewer zero observations. This suggests that Botrylloides became more established and colonies had more time to grow.

This suggests a progressive recolonization and development of Botrylloides populations over time:

  1. Early sampling periods are dominated by absence and small colonies.
  2. Later sampling periods show increased presence and larger colonies.
  3. The shift from small to large size classes indicates colony growth and persistence after panel gardening.
